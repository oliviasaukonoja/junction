      Why this works
      • A ladder, one step at a time. Graded exposure is the first-line treatment for specific phobias. You start where fear is real but manageable.
      • Test your prediction. Before each round you say what you fear will happen and how sure you are. After, you check what actually happened. That surprise is what your brain learns from.
      • No safety behaviours, and name the feeling. Looking away or holding the exit keeps the fear alive. Saying “I feel scared” out loud helps.
      • Never the same twice. Each round changes size, place and angle, so what you learn carries over to real life.
      • Needles: stop the fainting first. Applied tension keeps your blood pressure up, so you learn it before the exposure.
      • Scans: know what happens. Information and rehearsal with the real scanner sounds reduce MRI anxiety.

      A practice tool, not a medical device. If fear is severe, or you have fainted and hurt yourself, work with a clinician.
